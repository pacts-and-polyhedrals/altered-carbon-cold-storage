# A City That Remembers Selectively

## The Rainline tonight

The Rainline occupies the flood-shadow beneath Bay City's wealth. Rain rattles against stacked walkways. Clinic queues cross food queues. An old civic monument now supports an advertisement promising a second life at a monthly rate. People recognise sleeves before they recognise the people inside them, which makes a police chassis, a labour clone or a luxury body a form of involuntary introduction.

The city should feel inhabited, not uniformly vicious. A street cook feeds a synthetic customer broth the customer cannot taste because sitting together still matters. A child repairs an obsolete umbrella with packaging from a clone clinic. A tram driver waits three extra seconds for an elderly body. The rulers' power is frightening because it occupies systems on which decent people depend.

Three public images recur: Adria Sen washing a patient's hands, Severin Holt closing a law book, Oren Thale opening a transit gate. Their campaign line is **"Continuity is a promise."** Use it once in the ward, once on the street and once in the core. Do not make every background screen ominously wink at the party.

## Geography without a battle map

Ward Nine sits below a disused clinic on the east flood channel. Its service exit reaches the Night Stair market. From there, the Quiet Archive occupies a dry chapel to the north, Blue Lotus operates beneath a shuttered bathhouse to the west, and Sable Needle's Relay Seventeen stands above the canal to the south. Anansi House is a short ride inland. The Spire's public foundation is reachable by tram; its maintenance descent connects to the same canal tunnels.

Treat each move as roughly ten minutes in the fiction. None creates an automatic real-world timer. Anansi House can precede any street lead; its dossiers give directions and introductions. If the players go straight to the Spire, use the admission route in [[G19|Spire Entry]] and stage the archive evidence in the waiting chamber. Do not punish efficient deduction by hiding mandatory scenes behind them.

## Chronology

| Date | What actually happened |
|---|---|
| 2124-2251 | The pregens' earlier sleeves intersected with exploitation, hidden copying, abusive conditioning and identity laundering. These are precursor institutions, not a fully operating AI government. |
| 2248-2268 | Vey brought clinical memory repair, legal continuity certification and AI interface research into the same programme. Human investors expected a private longevity product. |
| 2269-2273 | Three civic roots obtained prototype organic interfaces and manufactured biographies. The investigators found connected irregularities. |
| 2274 | The team assembled its case, split it into redundant shares, sabotaged extraction and was captured under a sealed continuity order. |
| 2275-2383 | The programme became the Continuity Foundation. The three roots consolidated city power through nominally separate offices and bodies. Contacts changed sleeves, allegiances and names. |
| 2384, tonight | The seal and root lease reach renewal. Serin intervenes. The six selected PCs wake. The case becomes actionable again. |

Use the precise 110-year interval. A contact can have lived through that interval without staying loyal, remaining romantically available or possessing perfect recall.

## Factions as leverage

**The Continuity Foundation** offers clinical stabilisation, restored legal identity and access to preferred sleeves. Its clinic staff are mostly ordinary people. Its directors conceal the programme. A deal with the Foundation can save patients while protecting perpetrators; those are different outcomes.

**The Protectorate Continuity Office** can freeze a warrant or classify a witness as property. Asha wants custody without stack destruction. Iris Tan and Tom Arlen can help establish an accountable alternative. June Halden wants admissible proof, but fears her earlier signature will be discovered.

**Sable Needle Corporation** has relay logs and an administrator route into the Spire. Lena Orr can extract a cold maintenance record. Senior managers prefer a quiet settlement. Keeping transit and power technicians safe is the practical foundation of a non-catastrophic ending.

**Blue Lotus Combine** can move stacks and conceal sleeves. Niko Saint sells introductions and has a delivery ledger linking the Foundation to prohibited substrates. Hale Mercer has traded the party's location for money but also kept a second escape route open. Neither fact automatically makes Hale irredeemable.

**The Quiet Archive** preserves testimony, consent and chains of custody outside compulsory spin-up. Father Ash and Deacon Marr will help living witnesses regardless of faith. They will not resurrect a reserve stack without permission. Their refusal is a boundary, not an obstacle to solve by persuasion.

**The Chainbreakers** can secure exits and mobilise independent witnesses. Brother Cinder wants liberation but is tempted to reuse Palimpsest to remove coercion. He must be asked how consent will survive that project. He will accept destroying the editing engine while preserving voluntary treatment.

## Who knows what

Serin knows the interface exists, not the complete identity of all three rulers. Marlowe knows the historic route and suspicious service-root patterns, not what current bodies contain. Niko knows the cargo manifests. Asha knows the suppression order. Lena knows the root certificates are older than the patrons' asserted biographies. Vey knows the full truth. The three rulers know their own origins and each other's present identities, but disagree on what those origins permit them to do.
