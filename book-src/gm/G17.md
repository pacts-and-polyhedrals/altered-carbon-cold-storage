# Memory Node B: The Operating Theatre

**Within SC05: about 8-15 minutes.** Spotlight: Edda, Sabine and Anik; substitute any PC who cares about medicine, money or personhood. Fray: no automatic pulse. Handout: H08.

## The record

> There is an operating table, but no patient lies on it. The patient is represented by a branching light on the wall. A living clone waits in a suspension cradle beside it, breathing before anybody has been placed inside.
>
> A clinical voice asks, "Does an origin determine whether consent is necessary?"
>
> Someone using Edda Sorn's credentials answers, "No. Which is why you cannot omit it."

This is a legitimate excerpt from the original trial record. The interface patient is labelled only by a client code. Do not announce that the light is an AI unless the party already has proof or asks for a reasoned technical interpretation. It is a nonstandard continuity architecture; that supports the theory without completing it.

## Two consent documents

Give [[H08|H08: Clinical Exception]]. The first document permits Edda to place a protective veil over **her own case-memory index** as an act of sabotage. It explicitly excludes permission over other people. The second, later document claims the entire group requested collective clinical suppression. Its witness block was copied from an unrelated admission form.

Serin, observing or present by recorded statement, admits she certified the second document under pressure. She knew it overstated consent. Vey argued that the intervention was necessary to preserve the subjects for eventual repair. Her choice helped keep the party recoverable and also helped imprison them. Both can be true.

## The roleplay

Ask Edda what her earlier self hoped the veil would protect. Offer two plausible answers if the player is unsure: preserving the audit route against extraction, or preventing an involuntary copy from being used against the other witnesses. The player can propose another consistent reason. Do not force them to confess to harming the group.

Ask Sabine what responsibility belongs to someone whose money helped fund research later abused. The old financial records show the investment, not foreknowledge of every crime. Ask Anik whether a digital mind deserves informed consent even if it was built rather than born. These questions prepare the finale without deciding it.

Vey's recorded voice says, "A patient whose wishes prevent successful treatment cannot be the only authority over that treatment." The PCs may challenge the statement now. Their response helps you tailor his later offer; it does not change the archived past.

## Technical facts without a mandatory roll

The body in the cradle is organic and purpose-grown. There is no unwilling original human occupant displaced from that specific blank clone. The interface translates a nonstandard runtime into the sleeve's sensory and autonomic systems. A bank of harvested human life-history templates supplies believable autobiographical context. Some donors consented to narrowly defined therapeutic use; others were coerced or never asked. Repurposing those templates for political identities was outside every shown donor agreement.

Those facts make the crime concrete. Do not claim that tissue type determines personhood or that artificial minds are inherently incapable of feeling. The dangerous act is the appropriation of people and authority.

## Optional skill rewards

**Medicine or Science D0:** distinguish the experimental interface from a normal DHF transfer. Success identifies the separate hardware attestation required to link a runtime to a current organic body. That earns an automatic safe assay at the core without trusting Vey to interpret it. On failure, Serin identifies the correct test but must supervise it there.

**Data Analysis D0:** compare template-use contracts. Success extracts a victim-safe sample demonstrating use outside consent. This gives the final publication operation an automatic redaction contribution. Failure still obtains the facts; manual redaction can be done in the finale.

**Read Person D0**, directed at the live Serin rather than a recording: success reveals that she is prepared to testify against herself. It cannot force forgiveness or establish every historical fact.

## A tempting offer

The archive displays an optional **HARMONISE** treatment simulation. It promises fewer contradictions by selecting one dominant autobiographical pattern. Selecting Preview is safe and read-only. The preview shows what would be lost: a chosen attachment would retain its factual record but lose emotional ownership. It is not a guaranteed cure, and it cannot undo consent violations retroactively.

A PC who wants to test a deeper recall uses the standard overreach cost and learns Vey's own dependency: his place in the programme requires the patrons' continued belief that only he can maintain the interface. That vulnerability yields one automatic negotiation concession from him later. No treatment is performed without a separate explicit choice.

A requisition carries three signatures entered by software processes, followed by human countersignatures. The processes predate the manufactured client biographies. Marlowe says, "We now know there was an interface and a false history. The final record should tell us where the original witnesses intended to take their case."
