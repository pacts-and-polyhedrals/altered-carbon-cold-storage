# Memory Node A: The Court of Unreliable Witnesses

**Within SC05: about 10-15 minutes.** Spotlight: Nia, Imani and Solenne; substitute any PC who values duty or testimony. Fray: scheduled pulse 3. Handout: H07.

## The record

> Eight chairs face an empty judge's bench. In each chair sits the outline of a body you once occupied. A clerk reads a verdict in a voice assembled from your own.
>
> "The subjects collectively authorised isolation. Subject Voss closed the evacuation gate. The resulting losses were an accepted operational cost."
>
> On the clerk's desk, a second timestamp continues blinking beneath the first.

Tell the players that this is the official accusation, not the GM declaring what they did. The safe Compare control exposes two sources. One is an edited interview summary with a later timestamp. The other is a physical gate relay captured during the raid. Give [[H07|H07: Gate Comparison]].

The relay shows that Imani shut the gate **after** civilians passed through and **before** the retrieval unit reached them. It isolated armed pursuers, not evacuees. Asha then diverted a second team through a service door. The capture was not proof that Imani betrayed the party. The exact motive Imani felt remains the player's interpretation; the sequence is verifiable.

## The conversation

A recorded interrogator asks, "Was the loss of your own exit worth saving people who did not know your names?" Invite Imani or another selected PC to answer now. The archive records no new confession. Other PCs can ask what they believed about Imani during their final hours and whether they want to correct it.

Nia can question why the court treated a jointly produced clinical certificate as eight separate consents. Solenne can ask how a broadcast converts an accusation into a public memory. A less talkative player can choose which of two documents goes into the evidence packet and explain one reason.

**Do not ask the group to vote on whether Imani is a good person.** The productive question is what they do with an allegation once they know it was built on a false sequence.

## The continuity pulse

Offer pulse 3 after the comparison. An earlier self may remember being abandoned; the present record shows protection. That mismatch is enough to create Fray without revealing a secretly evil PC. Use Discipline for immersed participants, Composure for external viewers. The default D0/D1 thresholds follow the Fray track.

An accepted echo can produce a truthful additional line: Imani heard somebody inside the service door use the phrase "dirty mirrors" shortly before the team entered. That links Vey to the capture plan but is corroborative, not the sole proof.

## Optional skill rewards

**Investigation D0:** identify which part of the accusation was inserted later. Success supplies the responsible office's countersignature, giving leverage over Asha or June. Failure still leaves the gate sequence clear.

**Bureaucracy D0:** frame a formal objection using the differing timestamps. Success produces an audit petition Iris can use for lawful Spire admission. Failure means a more limited petition; the maintenance and custody routes remain.

**Discipline D1:** hold the viewpoint steady long enough to isolate the recorded interrogator's reflection. Success reveals Elias North's current presentation layered over an older Lucan Vey interface. Failure ends the optional close-up; it does not cause a further automatic pulse.

## Voluntary overreach

A PC may ask to recover the feeling beneath the revised interview. Warn of +2 Fray and raw 1d6 Ego loss. The reward is a recorded private instruction from Asha: **"No stack disposal. Keep the eight available for review."** This wins one automatic concession from Asha later: a witnessed rather than private transfer, or withdrawal of one security team. It does not erase her responsibility for the raid.

## The node's exit

The empty judge's bench lights up with three beneficiary codes. They are the same M9, J4 and A12 found on the delivery and relay records. They appear in a category labelled **FINAL CLIENT REVIEW**, not a human judge's name. Let the players notice the pattern automatically. This proves coordination between the case and the clients, not yet artificial origin.

Marlowe says, "The accusation has an author. Now we should examine the consent it claimed to represent." Proceed to [[G17|the Operating Theatre]].
