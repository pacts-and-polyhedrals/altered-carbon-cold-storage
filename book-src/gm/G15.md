# SC05: Palimpsest Virtuality

**35 / 45 / 55 minutes total, not per node.** Purpose: reconstruct the interrupted investigation and test the difference between memory, culpability and consent. Evidence: E05 and E06; supporting clues towards E07. Fray: pulses 3 and 4 only, unless a player chooses overreach. Full external replay is supported.

## Entering the archive

> The room does not disappear. It becomes two rooms occupying the same place. Anansi's damp wallpaper remains behind a clean white courtroom. Your present hand rests beside the remembered shape of another hand.
>
> Marlowe's voice says, "This is a reconstruction. It can show you a record. It cannot decide what that record means about you. The exit remains where we placed it."

Participants choose whether the old sleeve is shown as a body, a silhouette, a nameplate or simply a first-person voice. Outside observers see the same record and can speak into the shared channel. The current PCs remain the agents of the scene; recorded past selves do not issue binding commands.

## The three nodes

The **Court of Unreliable Witnesses** compares an official accusation with a physical gate record. The **Operating Theatre** separates Edda's actual consent from the later group certification and shows evidence of a nonstandard client interface. The **Last Broadcast** reveals the archive's redundant structure and the audit route the earlier party failed to use before capture.

For the shortest schedule, spend roughly ten minutes on each node and five minutes on entering and summarising. For longer play, expand personal questions rather than adding more technical rooms. The archive is not a dungeon requiring separate initiative, navigation or a puzzle password at every door.

## Rules for interacting

Marlowe provides three safe controls: pause, compare and exit. Using them needs no check. Data Analysis D0 can establish the provenance of a particular record; success grants an extra useful detail, while failure leaves the independent record's stated facts intact. Discipline governs attempts to alter the viewing environment, not the truth of the source.

The archive is write-protected. A PC can change how a scene is displayed without changing what happened. An intrusive deep recall may reveal context unavailable to the camera, but it uses the [[G04|voluntary overreach]] cost only after explicit warning. Give its reward in the node where it is offered; do not require it for E05, E06 or the final reveal.

## The hostile voice

Vey has a dormant diagnostic callback in the archive. It becomes visible as soon as the first node finishes: a fourth window labelled **THERAPEUTIC INTERVENTION REQUEST**. It cannot secretly overwrite a PC through the read-only channel. If opened, Vey appears as Elias North and offers relief from the distress the records cause.

> "You do not need to carry every version of a life. There is a clinical solution. I can stop the argument inside your heads."

He refuses to promise that all attachments will survive his treatment. Edda, Serin or any attentive PC can ask the decisive question: "Who selects the version that remains?" He answers, "A clinician has to make judgments." That is a philosophical threat and a negotiating clue, not a new forced damage event.

The group can close the window, record him, negotiate or ask a question. If they grant him read access to current identity data, he learns their present objectives and gains bargaining information at the council. Announce that before consent. Merely hearing him is not a hack.

## If technical resistance is desired

A Recovery Dipper may attempt to disrupt the display after the second node, but only if the party has opened Vey's bidirectional diagnostic channel. State the newly enabled risk. One successful Data Engineering or Discipline D0 check isolates it. A miss forces the group to disconnect and continue from the cached external replay; no unique clue is lost. Use ADV01 only if the players choose a sustained virtual confrontation, and do not run it in addition to an already extended memory scene.

## End-of-scene summary

Ask the players to finish three sentences: "The official version changed..."; "The old group was trying to..."; "Tonight we still need to prove..." Correct factual misunderstandings gently using the handouts. Do not correct their moral conclusions.

They now possess a valid audit route, a reason to confront the Foundation, and a more accurate account of their previous selves. The theory that the patrons are embodied AIs may be strong, but the complete three-pillar proof belongs in the core. Move to [[G19|Spire Entry]].
